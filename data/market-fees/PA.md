# Pennsylvania (PA) — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (scarce market) — 3 verified sources (1 rating-bureau regulatory manual + 2 provider-published settlement-fee sources), 2026-07-22

Pennsylvania is a **rating-bureau-uniform, all-inclusive-rate state**: title insurance premiums are
set by the Title Insurance Rating Bureau of Pennsylvania (TIRBOP), a nonprofit rating organization
licensed by the PA Insurance Department under The Insurance Company Law of 1921 (40 P.S.
section 910-41). TIRBOP's own current Manual (approved by the Department, amended through
08/01/24) states in its very first definitions section that the regulated "Charge" **includes**
"premium, examination and settlement or closing fee and every other Charge" (Section 1.6) — i.e.,
under PA's default "Company or Agent Procedure," the promulgated Sale Rate/Non-Sale Rate is a
single, rating-bureau-uniform number that legally bundles the title premium together with search,
examination, escrow, and settlement services. This was independently confirmed by cross-checking
the current 08/01/24 manual against the prior 04/01/23 vintage (identical Sale/Non-Sale/Approved-
Attorney rate tables — only endorsement-form additions changed) and against a third, older
(August 2019) Old Republic Title/Carbon Search and Settlement Services Inc. republication of the
same Sale/Non-Sale table (byte-for-byte identical base rates: $569.00/$512.00 at $0-$30,000
liability), meaning the promulgated rate table has been stable for 5+ years. **However — and this
is the central finding of this survey** — TIRBOP's own Manual carves out exactly the unregulated
service-fee territory the task brief anticipated, through two distinct mechanisms documented
below, and provider-published evidence of that unregulated territory turned out to be genuinely
scarce despite an exhaustive search (40+ distinct query strategies, 35+ direct provider-site
checks).

## Regulatory backbone (rating-bureau-uniform, not a market signal — except where it explicitly is)

- **Company or Agent Procedure Sale Rate** (owner's policies): $0-$30,000 = $569.00 flat; per-
  thousand additions above that (e.g., $100,000 = $1,025.00; $200,000 = $1,595.00; $1,000,000 =
  approx. $5,130.00). **Non-Sale Rate** (loan policies): $0-$30,000 = $512.00; $100,000 = $899.25;
  $200,000 = $1,383.25. Source: TIRBOP Manual Section 5.50, https://patitleratingbureau.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/TIRBOP-Manual-FINAL-APPROVED-08-01-2024-4857-2336-2733-v2.pdf.
- **Approved Attorney Procedure Rate** (Section 5.51): $0-$30,000 = $142.00 flat; $200,000 =
  $714.00 per the Manual's own worked example — dramatically lower than the Company/Agent Sale
  Rate ($1,595.00) at the same liability tier. This gap is the clearest quantitative evidence in
  this survey of how much of PA's "all-inclusive" rate is actually attributable to settlement
  services: the Approved Attorney Rate excludes them because, per Section 5.1.C, **"The charge for
  the search, examination of title and the settlement by the Approved Attorney is not governed by
  this Manual."** That sentence is the exact structural mechanism the task brief's "table
  funding/attorney-involvement-varies" note was pointing at — under one of PA's two legally defined
  closing procedures, the settlement charge is entirely unregulated by design.
- **Section 2.1(a)-(e)**: regardless of which procedure is used, the all-inclusive rate explicitly
  **excludes** (a) document preparation other than the commitment/closing statement/policy, (b)
  government recording charges, (c) overnight delivery, (d) bank wire transfer, and (e) receipt/
  printing of electronically-transmitted documents other than the commitment/closing statement/
  policy. These five categories are unregulated, separately chargeable by any PA provider type —
  the primary channel through which PA title companies actually compete on price even under the
  bundled Company/Agent Procedure.
- **Closing Protection Letter (CPL)**: $125.00 flat per transaction (Section 3.5), remitted
  entirely to the Insurer. **Escrow Service Charge**: $25.00 minimum for the first 6 months funds
  are held, plus $25.00 per additional year (Section 3.2). **Cancellation Charge**: $100.00 minimum
  (Section 3.1). **Record Owner and Lien Certificate**: $350.00 (1-4 family residential) or $750.00
  (other) per chain of title (Section 3.6).

ALT Title separately publishes a standalone **$250 Deed Preparation Fee**, and its own FSBO page

With only these two priced settlement-fee data points — one an exact flat fee, one a range, and
the two not describing a clean apples-to-apples comparable transaction type — there is no genuine
multi-point service-stack range to test for saturation stability, the same situation this survey
has previously documented for NM, IN, and VT (see PROGRESS.md). This is the central reason PA is
classified **scarce** rather than saturated despite a search effort that clears the saturation
floor's exhaustive-search bar many times over.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **PA's structural bundling requirement is the most legally explicit found anywhere in this
  survey.** Unlike FL's DFS-guidance-level bundling convention (a regulatory preference for one
  non-itemized "closing services fee" line) or TX's simple absence of a settlement-fee filing
  requirement, PA's TIRBOP Manual states outright, in its statutory-citation-backed definitions
  section, that the "Charge" **is** premium-plus-settlement as a matter of the underlying Insurance
  Company Law of 1921 (40 P.S. section 910-41; "fee" defined at Section 701) — the bundling is
  written into the enabling statute itself, not just into rating-bureau practice.
- **The Approved-Attorney-Procedure carve-out is the single most important market-structure finding
  of this session.** It directly confirms the task brief's note that PA is a "table funding"/
  attorney-involvement-varies state: closings conducted by an Approved Attorney are priced on a
  materially lower TIRBOP rate specifically because the attorney's own search/exam/settlement fee
  is billed separately and is completely outside TIRBOP's jurisdiction. This survey was unable to
  locate a single PA closing-attorney firm that publishes its own flat settlement fee online (see
  search log) — every firm checked (Ament Law Group, Sommer Law Group, MPL Law Firm/LandServices,
  Tarasi & Tarasi, Herb & Winters, and a dozen further Philadelphia/Pittsburgh firms found via
  search) either offers a free consultation/quote-only model or publishes only qualitative
  "flat-fee" marketing language without a dollar figure — a genuine market-opacity finding on the
  attorney side specifically, distinct from (and arguably more opaque than) the title-agent side.
- **Section 2.1's five excluded-charge categories (doc prep, recording, overnight, wire, e-doc)**
  are, in principle, the one place every PA provider — Company/Agent or Approved Attorney — should
  be free to compete on price regardless of procedure. In practice, almost no provider checked this
  session published exact dollar figures for these items either; ALT Title is the sole exception,
  bundling them into its $375 flat fee and stating $0 additional buyer-side processing fees.
- **National-brand direct offices publish premium-only content or interactive calculators
  exclusively.** Every national-brand underwriter/agent page checked this session (Stewart Title's
  PA rate-calculator page, First American's PA fee-calculator tools, Old Republic Title's PA
  presence, Chicago Title/Fidelity National PA pages, WFG's 2014-vintage PA rate PDF) contains
  either the TIRBOP-restated premium table or a bare interactive-calculator link with no static
  settlement-fee fallback — the same pattern documented in nearly every other state surveyed to
  date in this project.

## Saturation/scarcity reasoning

This session located only **3 good sources** (1 regulatory + 2 provider-published), well below the
6-source saturation floor, despite **40+ distinct query strategies and 35+ direct provider-site
checks** — far exceeding the 8-strategy exhaustive-search threshold required to invoke the scarce-
market provision. PA's structural characteristics explain why: (1) the Company/Agent Procedure's
legally all-inclusive rate removes the ordinary commercial incentive most other states' providers
have to publish a standalone settlement-fee number, since that number is simply the TIRBOP premium
itself; (2) the Approved Attorney Procedure routes settlement pricing to individual attorneys, who
overwhelmingly do not publish flat fees online (the same attorney-market-opacity pattern seen in
MA, CT, VT, RI, WV, and KY in this survey); and (3) even the five explicitly unregulated fee
categories (doc prep, recording, overnight, wire, e-doc) that every provider type is legally free
to price and publish went almost entirely unpublished, with ALT Title the sole clear exception.
With only two priced settlement-fee data points that are not directly comparable (one flat $375
fee for a specific FSBO scenario, one general $1,000-$2,500 range), there is no genuine multi-
provider range whose stability could honestly be tested against the saturation criterion's 10%
threshold — the same "too few comparable data points to test" situation previously documented for
NM, IN, and VT. PA is marked **complete (scarce market)** on that basis. The clearest positive
finding of this session is not a pricing range but a structural one: TIRBOP's own Manual text
(Section 5.1.C) explicitly names the Approved Attorney's settlement charge as unregulated, giving
this state's "scarce" classification an unusually well-documented legal mechanism rather than a
mere absence of public information.

## Calculator harvest addendum (2026-07-25)

Separate from the published-schedule survey above (which remains **complete (scarce market)**),
this session harvested three provider quote calculators for the standard $500,000/$400,000
Philadelphia County purchase scenario — see PA.json entries marked `"basis": "calculator"` and
CALCULATORS.md for full technical detail:

1. **ALT Title** (`alttitle.com`) — its own WordPress "tiq" plugin calls a genuine unauthenticated
   JSON REST API (`/wp-json/tiq/v1/quote`). ALT's own ancillary fees (closing/notary/courier/wire/
   e-doc/broker admin) are all **$0.00** — a deliberate competitive positioning also visible in the
   tool's own "competitor range" comparison figures. CPL $125, lender's premium $2,735 (standard).
2. **TitleWorks** (`titleworks.com`) — embeds a shared third-party platform, MyTitleRates.com
   (agency id `a=24`), driven via a single plain HTTP POST with no JS/auth. Produced the richest,
   most itemized HUD-1-style breakdown found in this survey: Document Prep $295, Notary $50,
   Courier $42.50, CPL $125, three $100 endorsements, Total Closing Costs $16,109 (buyer side,
   dominated by Philadelphia's transfer tax).
3. **Trident Land Transfer** (`tridentland.com`) — a second, distinct agency on the same
   MyTitleRates.com platform (`a=15`), confirming the platform reflects each agency's own real fee
   schedule rather than a shared template: E Doc Fee $125, Notary $55 (estimate), Wire $7.80,
   Overnight $20 — materially different from TitleWorks's own figures at the identical scenario.

This crosses the 3-provider calculator-quoted threshold for PA (see PROGRESS.md tracker). The
MyTitleRates.com discovery is significant beyond PA: it is shared SaaS infrastructure used by many
independent title agencies nationwide (each with its own `a=<id>`), analogous in importance to Old
Republic's ortconline.com postback tool for other states — see CALCULATORS.md's "For future
sessions" note for the recommended follow-up search strategy.
