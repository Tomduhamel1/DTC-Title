# West Virginia — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (scarce market) — 3 verified sources (all premium-only rate manuals), 2026-07-22

West Virginia is a filed-rate (insurer-filed and Commissioner-approved) title insurance market: West
Virginia Code §33-12-6b (implemented via Title 114, Series 77 of the West Virginia Legislative
Rules) requires every title insurer's non-commercial premium rate schedule to be filed with the
Offices of the Insurance Commissioner and to sit for a 60-day waiting period before taking effect,
and further requires filings to differentiate commercial from non-commercial rates. This session
found and verified **3 title-premium rate manuals** — Stewart Title Guaranty, WFG National, and
First National Title Insurance Company (FNTI) — all filed under that regime. West Virginia is also
an **attorney-closing state** (a licensed real estate attorney must be involved in every residential
closing), and this session's exhaustive search of both national-brand title companies and named WV
independent title/escrow companies and closing-attorney firms found **zero published settlement,
closing, escrow, doc-prep, or search/exam dollar figures** anywhere — matching the market-opacity
pattern already documented in AZ/CO/MI/MO/VA/AL/KY/ME/MS/WI/NE/ND/NH survey sessions. **21
distinct query strategies** plus direct checks of all 5 major national-brand underwriters/direct offices
(First American, Fidelity National/Chicago Title, Old Republic, Stewart, WFG) and 6 named
WV-specific independents/closing-attorney firms (BesTitle, Eastern Title, The West Virginia Title
Company, First Title & Escrow, Bailey & Slotnick, Ratified Title Group) yielded only the 3 usable
premium manuals. Marked **complete (scarce)** — comfortably past the 8-strategy threshold, well
below the 6-source saturation floor.

## All-in service-stack range observed

No comparable settlement/closing-service-fee range could be established this session. All 3 verified
sources are title-insurance-**premium**-only rate manuals whose own general provisions
affirmatively exclude settlement, escrow, closing, search, and examination charges from the filed
rate (see "Itemization / bundling patterns" below). The only non-premium dollar figures found
anywhere in West Virginia this session are the underwriters' own **Closing Protection Letter (CPL)
fees**:

- **Stewart:** $50 for a lender or purchaser, $75 for a seller (each party receiving the benefit is
  charged separately); an additional $50 if a second mortgage/HELOC is placed by a non-primary
  lender.
- **FNTI:** $50 for a lender, $25 for a purchaser, $25 for a seller.
- **WFG:** no standalone CPL fee section was located in the portions of the manual retrieved.

Stewart's and FNTI's CPL structures diverge notably on the purchaser/seller side ($50/$75 vs.
$25/$25) despite both being filed with the same regulator — a data point worth noting but far too
narrow (2 data points, one fee type) to constitute a testable settlement-fee range. General
secondary-source commentary (real-estate blogs, not counted as evidence) repeatedly cites a
$200-$900 settlement-fee range and $750-$1,250 flat attorney-closing fees for West Virginia, but no
such figure was traced to a provider's own published page this session, so none of it is recorded as
verified evidence.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **All 3 underwriter manuals explicitly and affirmatively exclude settlement/closing/escrow
  charges from the filed premium**, with FNTI's language the single clearest sentence found: "The
  rates set forth herein do not include any charge made for title search, title examination, closing,
  or escrow services performed by the Insurer, or any of its approved attorneys or agents. Charges
  made for such services are in addition to the rates and charges set forth herein." Stewart's
  definitions section similarly excludes "any settlement fee, trustee fee, attorney fee, surveying fee,
  inspection fee, document fee, closing fee, escrow fee or any other fee associated with escrow"
  from its defined "Charge." WFG's introduction names six excluded categories: "title search,
  surveys, closing services, settlement services, recording fees, other charges."
- **Stewart and FNTI both publish a standalone CPL fee schedule; WFG does not** (or at least none
  was found in the sections retrieved) — the same split pattern seen in several other scarce states
  surveyed this project.
- **All three underwriters price statewide with no metro/county tiering** — unlike states such as
  South Dakota (Stewart's Sioux Falls-metro vs. non-metro split) or Tennessee (All-Inclusive-Rate
  counties), none of Stewart, WFG, or FNTI differentiate West Virginia's 55 counties by rate tier for
  standard Owner's/Loan policies. FNTI's manual does publish a full 55-county numeric code table
  (for premium-remittance reporting purposes only, not differential pricing).
- **FNTI and Stewart both publish volume-tiered centralized/lender-special refinance rate
  programs** (FNTI's "Centralized Rates," Stewart's "Centralized Processing Loan and Refinance
  Rate," and WFG's "Lender's Special Rates" with 4 volume tiers) — all restricted to qualifying
  high-volume lenders using electronic centralized processing, and all substantially cheaper than
  the standard retail loan-policy rate at the same liability tier.
- **No independent WV title/escrow company or closing-attorney firm was found publishing any
  static dollar figure** for closing fee, settlement fee, title search/exam, doc prep, wire, courier,
  notary, or escrow. This is a genuine market-opacity finding, not a search-coverage gap: 6 named
  WV providers were checked directly (see below), and every one either has no pricing page at all or
  routes exclusively to a phone-consultation/quote-request contact.

## Metro / regional differences

No metro or regional pricing differentiation was found anywhere in West Virginia. All 3 verified
underwriter premium manuals are explicitly statewide with a single rate table applicable to all 55
counties. This session specifically searched the 4 target metro/regional areas named in the task
brief — **Charleston/Kanawha**, **Morgantown/Monongalia**, **Huntington/Cabell**, and the
**Eastern Panhandle (Berkeley/Jefferson, i.e. Martinsburg)** — and located real, named providers
operating in each (BesTitle has offices in Charleston, Morgantown, and Huntington; Bailey &
Slotnick has offices in Charleston and Teays Valley; Eastern Title serves Morgantown and
Huntington), but none of them publish a static fee schedule for their metro-specific office, so no
regional settlement-fee comparison could be constructed.

## Premium rate card (filed-rate state)

West Virginia is confirmed insurer-filed for non-commercial title premiums under West Virginia
Code §33-12-6b and Title 114, Series 77 of the West Virginia Legislative Rules: "No title insurer may
charge any rates after September 1, 2006 except in accordance with the premium rate schedule and
manual filed with and approved by the Commissioner," with rate schedules required to sit for a
60-day waiting period and to differentiate commercial from non-commercial pricing. The Offices of
the Insurance Commissioner's own summary PDF (wvinsurance.gov/Portals/4/pdf/title.pdf) returned
HTTP 503 on two separate fetch attempts this session and could not be retrieved directly; no public
SERFF-style searchable filing repository was located for West Virginia (unlike Kansas's or Idaho's
public filing databases), so all 3 verified premium schedules were located and fetched directly from
each underwriter's own website. At $100,000 of owner's-policy liability: Stewart = **$390** (4 x
$100 x $3.90/thousand... i.e., $3.90/thousand flat to $100k), WFG = **$400** ($4.00/thousand), FNTI
= **$400** at $100,000 (its first tier runs to $125,000 at the same $4.00/thousand rate) — a tight
2.5% spread between the cheapest (Stewart) and the two identical WFG/FNTI figures at this
coverage level. See WV.json for the full per-provider tier tables.
