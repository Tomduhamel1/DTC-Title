# Maryland (MD) Title / Closing / Settlement Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (scarce market) — 5 verified sources, 2026-07-21

Maryland is an insurer-filed title insurance state (not promulgated/bureau-rate), but
published settlement/service-fee schedules proved scarce despite an extensive search: 10+
distinct query strategies and direct checks of national underwriters, independent title
companies, and closing-attorney firms across Chicago-metro-equivalent submarkets (Baltimore,
DC suburbs/Montgomery County, Cecil County, Eastern Shore) turned up only 5 usable sources —
below the contract's 6-source saturation floor. Two of the five (Stewart, WFG) are
title-premium-only underwriter manuals that explicitly disclaim any settlement-fee content;
only three (Ardent Title, TPF Legal, CAL Settlements) actually price settlement/closing
services, and none of the large national-brand direct offices (First American, Old Republic,
Chicago Title, Fidelity National) were found to publish a static Maryland settlement-fee
schedule — every one offers only an interactive rate calculator or contact-for-quote page.
Maryland is marked **complete (scarce market)**.

## Observed settlement/service-stack range

Only 3 of the 5 sources price settlement services at all (the 2 underwriter manuals are
premium-only by design):

- **Ardent Title** (Cecil County): $395 core closing fee (bundling doc prep + consummation);
  full stack with title exam ($225), binder/CPL ($50), wire ($25), courier ($25), and
  notary ($10) ≈ **$730** on a standard purchase.
- **CAL Settlements** (Montgomery County/DC suburbs): $395 buyer settlement fee (bundling
  attorney fee + notary + doc prep); full buyer-side stack with binder ($125), title exam
  ($100), abstract/search (up to $325), CPL ($45), courier ($95), wire ($20), e-recording
  ($25) ≈ **$1,130-$1,355**; seller's side is a separate, much smaller $250 fee plus deed
  prep ($75).
- **TPF Legal** (closing attorney, unspecified metro): $750 flat rate, no itemization
  published (likely already inclusive of services Ardent/CAL itemize separately).

**Observed range: $395-$750 core settlement/closing fee; $730-$1,355 full itemized stack**
depending on submarket and whether ancillary services (survey, judgment report, release
tracking) apply. This is a narrower dollar range than several other states surveyed, but
the sample is small (3 pricing sources) and geographically scattered (Cecil County,
Montgomery County, and an unspecified-metro attorney firm), so it should be read as
indicative rather than saturated.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **Both underwriter manuals (Stewart, WFG) draw an unusually explicit, near-identical
  line** between title insurance premium and settlement services — WFG's introduction is
  the most complete disclaimer list found in any state surveyed to date (naming title
  search, abstracts, attorney's fees, surveys, escrow, closing services, settlement
  services, and recording fees all as excluded).
- **Buyer-side settlement fees bundle attorney/notary/doc-prep into one charge** at both
  independent providers found (Ardent $395, CAL Settlements $395) — a striking numeric
  coincidence between two unrelated Maryland firms in different counties.
- **CAL Settlements is the only source with an explicit buyer/seller fee split**: buyer
  pays the $395 settlement fee plus most itemized services, while seller pays a much
  smaller $250 settlement fee plus deed prep ($75) and mortgage-release tracking ($150/loan)
  — a cleaner allocation model than most other states surveyed, where a single settlement
  fee is typically split by custom rather than priced separately per side.
- **CPL pricing varies more than in other states**: WFG's underwriter-level CPL is a flat
  $30 (remitted entirely to the underwriter, not split with the agent); Stewart's is $50;
  CAL Settlements charges $45 for a First American CPL — no convergence on a single figure
  the way GA/IL saw across their underwriters.

## Calculator harvest addendum (2026-07-26)

Separate from the published-schedule survey above (which remains **complete (scarce
market)**), this session hunted provider quote calculators (as opposed to static fee
schedules) for the standard $500,000/$400,000 Montgomery County purchase scenario — see the
MD.json entry marked `"basis": "calculator"` and CALCULATORS.md for full technical detail.

1. **Federal Title & Escrow Company** (`tools.federaltitle.com/titleagents/QuickQuote/`) —
   a genuine first-party ASP.NET WebForms calculator (DC/MD/VA-licensed independent title
   company, Chevy Chase MD), driven end-to-end via plain HTTP POST with no browser, no auth,
   and no personal data (the tool's own copy states "No login or personal information
   required"). Montgomery County is the calculator's own default MD county — no substitution
   needed. Returned a fully itemized buyer/seller settlement statement: Settlement/Closing
   Fee $975 (buyer) / $550 (seller), CPL $30, Title Search/Abstract Fee $275, deed and
   mortgage recording fees $60 each, plus state/county transfer and recordation taxes; total
   Title Fees $3,563 (buyer) / $985 (seller). One EventValidation gotcha was hit and resolved:
   a `rblRepeatClient` radio control present on the initial page load is absent from the
   post-state-selection re-render, so submitting any value for it crashes the postback with an
   HTTP 500 — omitting the field entirely (matching the currently-rendered form) fixed it, the
   same "field for a control not in the current DOM breaks the postback" failure mode already
   logged for Old Republic's `ReoList` and Knight Barry's Oregon county control.

This is **one** working calculator for Maryland, short of the 3-provider calculator-quoted
threshold. A broad search (MyTitleRates.com, Knight Barry, Old Republic's ortconline.com,
and roughly a dozen independent MD agencies found via web search) turned up no further
harvestable calculators this session — see CALCULATORS.md for the full list of dead ends
investigated (mostly TitleCapture and Qualia Connect widget embeds, which remain jsOnly
platform-wide, plus several gated/account-required and third-party-aggregator sites ruled
out of scope). Maryland's calculator landscape is dominated by a small number of shared
third-party platforms (TitleCapture, Qualia Connect, TRGC PowerSnap, TitleClose) rather than
first-party tools, which is why the yield here is thinner than PA's — Federal Title is the
only MD-licensed provider found this session running its own uncontrolled, stateless,
browser-free-drivable calculator.
