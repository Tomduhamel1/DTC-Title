# South Carolina (SC) — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: COMPLETE (scarce market) — 4 documents / 4 providers verified

4 documents verified across 4 providers: Stewart Title Guaranty (premium
manual + $25 CPL fee), WFG National (premium manual, rate-for-rate
identical to Stewart's despite an 11-year vintage gap), and 2 closing-
attorney firms in different metro areas -- Mogil Law Firm (Hilton Head/
Beaufort-Jasper Counties, a genuine full itemized settlement-fee schedule
dated 2025-04-01) and Ingram Law Firm (Cheraw/Chesterfield County, 3
ancillary lender-triggered fees, base combined fee quote-only). This is a
richer find than most "scarce" states in this survey -- 2 of 4 sources are
genuine non-premium settlement-fee schedules from different SC metros --
but still short of the 6-source saturation floor despite an extensive
search. Marked **complete (scarce)**.

## Key market structure finding

South Carolina requires a licensed attorney to supervise every real estate
closing (a statutory requirement, unlike Rhode Island's customary-only
practice), and closing attorneys commonly handle title search, escrow, and
settlement functions directly -- similar in structure to Georgia, Alabama,
and Kentucky's attorney-closing markets, though notably more transparent:
2 different SC closing-attorney firms in this survey (Mogil in the
Lowcountry, Ingram in the Pee Dee/upstate-adjacent region) do publish
actual dollar-denominated fee information, unlike the near-total opacity
found in AL/KY/MS/ME.

Both national-underwriter rate manuals found (Stewart 2022, WFG 2011)
contain textually near-identical "General Rules" language excluding
"settlement fee, trustee fee, attorney fee, surveying fee, inspection fee,
document fee, closing fee, escrow fee or any other fee associated with
escrow" from the premium charge -- and both separately acknowledge that "a
work/preparation charge may be collected in addition to the applicable
premium" for preparing a title commitment, "retained by the agent/agency/
entity" and "disclosed at or prior to closing," confirming this ancillary
commitment fee is a genuine, real, market-standard charge in South
Carolina even though neither manual quantifies its amount (that is set and
disclosed by the individual closing agent, not the underwriter). South
Carolina has no title insurance rating bureau (unlike NC/OH/LA/OR
discovered elsewhere in this survey) -- Stewart's and WFG's premium
schedules are independently filed, yet happen to be dollar-for-dollar
identical across every liability tier despite an 11-year gap between the
two documents' effective dates, a striking (and likely coincidental or
informally benchmarked) cross-underwriter convergence.

## Observed service-stack range

Using Mogil Law Firm's fully dated (2025-04-01) schedule as the primary
comparable: a Hilton Head-area purchase settlement fee alone ranges
**$650 (lot purchase, no lender) to $1,000 (single-family purchase, with
lender)**, with refinance settlement fees running **$600-$900**. Adding
Ingram's ancillary lender-triggered fees ($100 commitment + $35 CPL, when
both apply) to a comparable Cheraw-area transaction would add roughly
$135 on top of its own (undisclosed) base fee -- not directly comparable to
Mogil's all-in figures since Ingram's core fee is unquantified. With only
one fully quantified schedule (Mogil) and one partially quantified
schedule (Ingram) from two different, non-adjacent SC regions, no
statistically meaningful statewide range or saturation check can be
performed -- consistent with the scarce-market determination.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- Both Mogil and Ingram explicitly separate their settlement/closing fee
  from title insurance premium ("exclusive of title search, title
  insurance" — Mogil; "does not include title insurance premiums or fees"
  — Ingram), matching Stewart's and WFG's manual-level exclusion language
  -- a rare case where independent attorney-firm disclosures and
  underwriter manual language corroborate each other on the same
  structural point.
- Both national underwriters converge on an unquantified-but-acknowledged
  "commitment work/preparation charge" retained by the local
  agent/attorney -- effectively the underwriters admitting the settlement-
  fee layer exists and is priced by the closing agent, not by them.
- Mogil's schedule shows a customary buyer/seller-pays split by
  transaction leg (Seller customarily pays the CL 100 termite report and
  deed stamps; Buyer customarily pays the Hilton Head transfer tax absent
  contrary negotiation) -- a Lowcountry-specific customary-cost allocation
  pattern.
- Stewart's and WFG's CPL fees differ in structure: Stewart's is $25 flat
  (with a possible additional charge for a second mortgage/CPL on the same
  transaction); WFG's 2011 SC manual does not mention a CPL fee at all (a
  coverage gap, possibly because CPLs became more standardized in the
  underwriting industry after 2011).

## Premium rate cards

See Verified sources #1-2 above (Stewart 2022, WFG 2011). Both manuals'
Basic Schedule of Charges rate tables are dollar-for-dollar identical
across every liability tier ($3.60/$3.00/$2.10/$1.80/$1.20 per $1,000),
despite the 11-year gap between effective dates and South Carolina having
no rating bureau to enforce uniformity.

## Metro differences

Mogil Law Firm (Hilton Head Island/Beaufort-Jasper Counties, Lowcountry)
and Ingram Law Firm (Cheraw/Chesterfield County, Pee Dee region) are the
only 2 metro/regional data points found; their fee structures aren't
directly comparable (Mogil publishes a full quantified schedule, Ingram
only ancillary fees), but Mogil's disclosed Hilton Head Island Transfer
Tax (0.25% of purchase price, customarily Buyer-paid) is a distinctly
Lowcountry-specific charge not found in any other South Carolina source.

## Calculator harvest addendum (2026-08-09) — Fidelity National Title (FNF) gains SC's 2nd provider

**2 of 3 calculator-basis providers — still below 3-provider threshold** (prior: 1 — WFG). See SC.json's newest `basis: "calculator"` entry for full itemized figures and methodology.

The 2026-08-06 session logged SC's FNF harvest as blocked by an "unsolved AmountLoan1 postback quirk" (the server silently dropping the loan-amount field, alongside the same finding for UT). This session found the real cause: a bug in that session's own replay script, not a server-side block — every extracted ASP.NET hidden-field dict was including the page's `<input type="submit">` button name/value pairs (e.g. `btnGeneralNext=Next`) and carrying them forward unchanged into every later POST, causing the server to treat each subsequent postback as an implicit re-click of that button and desync the wizard state in a way that looked exactly like a silently-dropped field. Fixing the replay script to only send a submit button's name/value on the request actually "clicking" it resolved the issue immediately — see UT.md's parallel addendum for the same fix applied there (UT crossed its 3-provider threshold as a result; SC did not, since it only gains a 2nd provider here).
