# Vermont — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (scarce market) — 6 verified sources (3 premium-only rate manuals + 3 settlement/search-fee-adjacent sources), 2026-07-22

Vermont, like Massachusetts and Connecticut, is an **attorney-closing state**: real
estate closings are conducted by licensed attorneys, and title insurance is issued
"by or through" the closing attorney (Peet Law Group's own page states the attorney
"will likely receive 70% of the total premium"). This session confirmed that
hypothesis directly and found **no independent title/escrow company layer of the
kind seen in title-company states** -- the handful of Vermont companies calling
themselves "title" companies (Green Mountain Title, Vermont Title Search/Property
Title of Vermont, TitleXCo) are wholesale **title-search/abstract vendors that sell
into the attorney-closing pipeline**, not retail settlement agents competing with
attorneys for the closing itself. Vermont's title insurance premium market is
filed/published by all three national underwriters with an active public presence
(First National Title Insurance Company/FNTI, WFG National, Stewart Title Guaranty),
plus CATIC/Vermont Attorneys Title Corporation (VATC) which dominates the market as
"the #1 underwriter of title insurance in Vermont" but whose own rate book and
state-resources page were **both blocked with HTTP 403** on every attempt this
session. Despite **32 distinct query strategies** plus direct checks of every major
national-brand underwriter (First American, Old Republic, Chicago Title/Fidelity
National, plus the three that did yield manuals), CATIC/VATC, and roughly a dozen
named Vermont closing-attorney firms and independent title/abstract companies, this
session found only **6 usable sources** with actual dollar figures. Marked
**complete (scarce)**: while the raw count reaches the numeric "at least 6"
threshold used for saturation, the 3 non-premium sources found are three
structurally different fee types (a bundled attorney all-in closing fee, an hourly
search rate, and an itemized search-product price list) that cannot be combined
into one comparable "all-in service-stack range" the way a true saturation check
requires -- the same "no service-stack range exists to test saturation" situation
documented for Indiana earlier in this survey. See "Search log" below for the full
methodology.

## Observed fee ranges (no single comparable "all-in" range exists)

Vermont's evidence splits into two categories that never overlap in the same
document, and the settlement-side category itself splits further into three
non-comparable pricing models:

**Title insurance premium (filed, national underwriters), at $200,000 owner's-policy
liability:**
- FNTI (2024-09-17): $260.00 base + $3.25/thousand from $50,000-$1,000,000 →
  **$748.00** at $200,000 (formula: $260 + $3.25 × 150 = $747.50, rounded to $748
  per the manual's own rounding rule).
- WFG (2022-05-01): $245.00 base + $3.25/thousand over $50,000 = $732.50, rounded
  up to **$733** at $200,000 per the manual's fractional-dollar rounding rule.
- Stewart (2013-08-01 vintage): direct table lookup shows **$748** Owner's Premium
  at $200,000 (matching FNTI's formula almost exactly, likely because FNTI licenses
  or mirrors an older common-ancestor rate structure).

  These three converge within roughly **2%** of each other at $200,000 liability --
  a tight cross-underwriter spread similar to Utah's finding earlier in this survey,
  though here it likely reflects Stewart's and FNTI's near-identical rate-table
  formulas (both use a $260 base + $3.25/thousand structure) rather than true
  independent convergence.

**Settlement/closing-service fees (excluding title premium) -- three
non-comparable pricing models, by source:**
- **Peet Law Group (closing attorney, bundled all-in fee):** buyer purchase
  $1,400-$1,750; seller (deed prep) ~$1,000; refinance $1,200-$1,500. This is the
  only genuine **all-in** settlement-fee figure found this session -- it bundles
  title search, examination, document preparation, lender coordination, and the
  closing itself into one attorney fee, consistent with Vermont's attorney-closing
  custom.
- **Green Mountain Title (title-search vendor, hourly):** $65.00/hour standard,
  $100.00/hour rush, plus mileage/costs -- a wholesale search-labor rate, not a
  flat per-transaction fee. A typical multi-hour search might run roughly
  $130-$325 at the standard rate, but no flat equivalent is published.
- **Vermont Title Search/Property Title of Vermont (title-search vendor, itemized
  retail products):** $75-$675 (starting prices) across ~25 distinct search-depth
  products (e.g., $175 residential current-owner search, $350 30-year full search,
  $675 50-year easement search).

Because the only genuine all-in bundled figure (Peet Law's $1,000-$1,750) has no
second comparable data point from another closing-attorney firm, and the two
title-search vendors price on entirely different models (hourly vs. itemized
flat-per-product), **no market-wide "all-in service-stack range" can be
constructed or tested for saturation** the way this project does for
title-company states. This mirrors Indiana's finding earlier in the survey.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **All three national underwriters with a public rate manual (FNTI, WFG, Stewart)
  explicitly and affirmatively exclude settlement/closing/escrow charges from
  their filed premiums.** FNTI's exclusion (Section F, "Risk Rate"): "The rates
  set forth herein do not include any charge made for title search, title
  examination, closing, or escrow services performed by the Insurer, or any of
  its approved attorneys or agents." WFG's (Section 1, Introduction): rates "do
  not include charges for title search, surveys, escrow, closing services,
  settlement services, recording fees, other charges." Stewart's (General
  Provisions): "do not include any fee made for title search, abstract fees,
  title examination, settlement, closing, or escrow services performed by
  approved attorneys, agents, or offices."
- **CPL fees are nearly uniform across the two underwriters that publish them**:
  FNTI charges $25.00 per transaction (plus an extra $50.00 if a second
  mortgage/HELOC involves a non-primary lender); WFG charges a flat $25.00 per
  issuance covering all protected parties. Both cite the same statutory basis
  (Vermont Statutes Annotated Title 8 and Vermont Insurance Division Bulletin
  108). Stewart's manual does not itemize a standalone CPL fee.
- **Vermont's attorney-closing custom bundles what other states itemize
  separately.** Where a Massachusetts or Connecticut closing-attorney fee page
  (per prior sessions in this survey) sometimes breaks out doc prep, courier, and
  recording as separate line items, Peet Law Group's published fee page quotes a
  single all-in range per transaction type (buyer/seller/refinance) with no
  itemized doc-prep, wire, courier, notary, or recording sub-fees disclosed
  anywhere on the firm's site (its "Real Estate Closing Costs" and "What Is the
  Cost of Title Insurance in Vermont" pages, both fetched this session, list cost
  *categories* -- appraisal, recording, transfer tax, etc. -- with zero dollar
  figures, routing readers to CATIC's third-party rate calculator instead).
- **The two verified "title company" names in Vermont are wholesale search
  vendors, not retail settlement agents.** Green Mountain Title and Vermont Title
  Search/Property Title of Vermont both sell title-examination/abstract products
  to attorneys, lenders, and other professionals rather than conducting closings
  themselves -- a structural difference from title-company states where the
  "title company" *is* the settlement agent. This matches the pattern flagged in
  the task brief (Vermont resembling MA/CT) and confirms it directly rather than
  by inference.
- **Vermont Title Search's product list is unusually granular** for a "scarce"
  state: 25 distinct products spanning current-owner searches, two-owner
  searches, full 30/40-year searches, chain-of-title searches back to 1940,
  judgment/lien/UCC searches, easement searches (20/30/50-year), mineral-rights
  searches (20/30/50-year), preliminary/SBA searches, and document retrieval --
  each with its own starting price. This is a genuine, dollar-denominated,
  statewide (all 14 counties) fee schedule, even though it covers only the
  search/exam slice of a closing rather than the full settlement stack.

## Metro / regional differences

None of the 6 verified sources publish metro- or county-tiered pricing. All three
national-underwriter premium manuals are explicitly statewide ("for use in all
Counties of Vermont" / State = Vermont). Peet Law Group's attorney fee ranges
apply firm-wide across its Williston (Chittenden County -- Burlington metro) and
White River Junction (Windsor County) offices, with the firm stating it serves
clients "throughout the State of Vermont" -- no separate rate disclosed for either
office location. Green Mountain Title and Vermont Title Search both explicitly
serve all or nearly all Vermont towns/counties at the same statewide rate.
Task-brief target metros (Burlington/Chittenden County, Montpelier, Rutland,
Brattleboro) were each individually searched for a locally-distinct fee schedule
(see search log below); none surfaced separate metro pricing from any provider.

## Premium rate card (filed-rate state)

Vermont's title insurance premium market is insurer-filed. FNTI's, WFG's, and
Stewart's manuals were located and fetched directly from each underwriter's own
website (see VT.json for full tier tables). CATIC, described by multiple sources
as "the #1 underwriter of title insurance in Vermont" through its exclusive
Vermont agent, Vermont Attorneys Title Corporation (VATC), publishes a "Vermont
Title Insurance Premiums" rate book that is "on file with Vermont Department of
Financial Regulation" per CATIC's own rate-calculator disclaimer page -- but
neither the rate book itself (catic.com/sites/default/files/Portals/0/PDF/
VT_Ratebook.pdf, guessed by analogy to CATIC's confirmed New Hampshire ratebook
URL pattern) nor CATIC's Vermont state-resources page could be fetched; both
returned HTTP 403 Forbidden on every attempt this session. VATC's own public rate
page (vermontattorneystitle.com/rate-calculator/) is confirmed to be an
interactive calculator only, disclaiming that it is "subject to the terms,
conditions and rates disclosed in the most current edition of CATIC's ... rate
book," with the only static dollar figure on the page being an optional $50.00
CPL add-on. The Vermont Department of Financial Regulation's own property &
casualty rate-filing pages (dfr.vermont.gov) were referenced in search snippets
but not directly crawled for a public filing-search portal analogous to Kansas's
or Idaho's; a targeted DFR search (query 5 below) surfaced only general filing-
procedure pages, not a searchable filed-rate repository.

## Saturation / scarcity reasoning

The raw source count (6) numerically satisfies the completion contract's "at
least 6 good sources" saturation floor, and the query-strategy count (32) is far
past the "at least 8 distinct query strategies" scarcity floor -- so this state
sits at a genuine boundary between the two outcomes. The deciding factor is the
contract's saturation test itself: "the last 3 added did not move the observed
all-in service-stack range (min/max) by more than ~10%." That test presumes a
single, comparable range exists to test. Here it does not: of the 3 non-premium
sources, only Peet Law Group publishes a genuine **all-in** bundled closing fee
(and only one such firm was found, so there is no second data point to compare
it against for stability); Green Mountain Title and Vermont Title Search/Property
Title of Vermont price the *search-only* slice of a closing on two mutually
incompatible models (hourly vs. itemized flat-per-product) that cannot be summed
into a comparable "all-in" figure without an assumed search-duration estimate
that this survey's evidence rules forbid inventing. This is structurally
identical to the situation documented for Indiana earlier in this survey ("6
total documents but only 1 priced settlement-fee source... no service-stack
range exists to test saturation"), and for the same reason this session marks
Vermont **complete (scarce market)** rather than saturated, despite crossing the
raw 6-source count.
