# California Title & Escrow Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (saturated) — 6 verified sources, 2026-07-21

California meets the completion contract's saturation bar: 6 independently verified,
directly-fetched published sources, spanning independent title/escrow companies,
national-brand underwriters, and both title-only and title+escrow rate manuals. The
3 most recently added sources (Stewart Title Guaranty, Fidelity National Title, and
in large part WFG) are title-premium-only rate manuals with no escrow/settlement
fee schedule of their own — they corroborate the earlier finding (Corinthian) that
CA underwriters routinely publish premiums but not settlement fees, and did not
move the observed all-in service-stack range by more than ~10% (WFG's own escrow
table, the only new escrow-fee data among the three, sits inside the range already
established by Pacific Coast Title's zone table — see below). Per the saturation
rule, additional CA search effort is not expected to change the answer.

## Escrow/settlement fee range actually observed

Only 3 of the 6 verified CA sources price a settlement/escrow fee at all
(Corinthian, Stewart, and Fidelity are title-premium-only): **First American**,
**Pacific Coast Title**, and **WFG National**.

- **First American** (Region 3 / Bay Area, all-cash): $1,100 (up to $250,000) to
  $2,500 (over $2,000,000), rising in defined price steps; +$430 (one loan) /
  +$200 (each additional loan) New Loan Services fee if financed; +$500 Short
  Sale/FSBO surcharge.
- **Pacific Coast Title** (zone-tiered): Zone 7 (Alameda/Contra Costa/Santa Clara)
  $450 (up to $100,000) scaling to $1,700 (at $2,000,000, minimum thereafter);
  Zone 1 Orange County $700 (minimum) scaling to a **$6,500 minimum over
  $1,500,000** — the highest tier observed in CA; Zone 1 Riverside/San
  Bernardino and Zone 2 LA County $700 to a $4,500 minimum over $1,000,000; Zone
  10 San Diego $700 (base) to a $2,800 minimum over $700,000.
- **WFG National** (All Counties, Basic/All-Inclusive): $1,150/$1,550 (up to
  $100,000) scaling to $7,300/$7,700 (at $1.9M-$2M, then +$1.00/thousand above
  $2M) — the highest published ceiling found in CA, in the same order of
  magnitude as Pacific Coast Title's Orange County $6,500 floor for
  above-$1.5M transactions. WFG separately publishes materially lower rates for
  Northern CA counties (Alameda, Contra Costa, Fresno, Kern, Merced, San
  Joaquin, Santa Clara, Stanislaus): $780/$1,180 up to $2,238/$2,638 at $2M —
  roughly 30-40% below the All Counties default table at the same price point,
  confirming settlement fees vary sharply by county even within one company.

**All-in observed range: roughly $450 (Northern CA / Bay Area low tier) to
$7,700 (WFG All Counties, near $2M sale price, All-Inclusive tier)** — a wide
spread driven primarily by county/zone, not by which of the 6 companies is
compared, since the same company (Pacific Coast, WFG) shows 3-10x variation
across its own zone tables.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

The pattern from the earlier (thin-evidence) pass holds up under the fuller
6-source sample: **title insurance premiums are always itemized and published
in exact-dollar tiers; settlement/escrow fees are itemized far less
consistently, and half of California's largest underwriters (Stewart,
Fidelity, and functionally Corinthian) do not publish them at all** in their
public-facing rate manuals — settlement fees in CA appear to live primarily in
underwritten-title/escrow-company-specific filings (Pacific Coast Title/Gateway,
WFG's escrow division, First American's Region-specific guide) rather than in
the underwriter's own document, even when underwriter and escrow provider share
a brand (Stewart Title Guaranty vs. a Stewart-affiliated escrow agency; WFG
National Title Insurance Co. vs. its own escrow Section 10 — WFG is the outlier
that actually publishes both in one document).

Where settlement fees ARE published, the same bundling structure recurs across
all three pricing sources:
- The base sale/loan escrow fee is described as covering "ordinary" or "basic"
  services only — document prep, standard courier, standard overnight delivery,
  in-office signing, disbursement — with "additional charges" reserved for
  anything outside that scope, per near-identical statutory boilerplate citing
  California Insurance Code §12401.8 in all three documents.
- **WFG** is the only source in CA to itemize a long list (~20 items) of
  individually-priced "additional processing fees" separate from its bundled
  Basic/All-Inclusive escrow tiers: wire $15, e-doc $50, e-recording $23,
  overnight delivery $25 ($35 oversized), redraw $75, HOA processing $25,
  bankruptcy coordination $400, loan tie-in $200/loan, doc prep $50/document.
  This is the most granular fee-by-fee breakdown found in any state surveyed to
  date.
- **Pacific Coast Title** itemizes a smaller but still explicit list: e-doc
  $75/loan, notary/out-of-office signing $150/file, sub-escrow $90-$125, short
  sale processing $300-$500, 1031 exchange $250, funds-held-over-90-days $25/mo.
- **First American** is the least itemized of the three: only the New Loan
  Services fee ($430+$200/loan) and Short Sale/FSBO surcharge (+$500) are
  broken out; everything else (doc prep, courier, wire, notary) is folded
  silently into the base "Basic Escrow Services" tier or the undifferentiated
  "additional Charges may be made" catch-all.

## Title insurance premiums (filed-rate, for context)

CA premiums are filed per-insurer (not promulgated), and the fuller 6-source
sample confirms real divergence at matched price points:

| Sale/policy price | Corinthian Basic/CLTA | First American EAGLE Owner's | Stewart Title Guaranty CLTA Standard | Fidelity National CLTA Owner's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $500,000 | $1,480.00 | $1,746 | $1,512 | $1,457 |
| $1,000,000 | $2,280.00 | $2,723 | $2,342 | $2,241 |

Divergence at $500,000 spans roughly $1,457-$1,746 (~20% range) and at
$1,000,000 roughly $2,241-$2,723 (~22% range) across the four underwriters that
publish comparable owner's-policy tables — consistent with CA being a genuine
filed-rate (not promulgated) state.
