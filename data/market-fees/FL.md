# Florida — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (saturated) — 9 verified sources (1 regulatory promulgated-rate bundle + 8 provider-published settlement-fee schedules/documents), 2026-07-22

Florida is a **promulgated-rate state**: title insurance *premiums* are fixed statewide by the
Florida Office of Insurance Regulation under Fla. Stat. §627.782/§627.7825 and Florida
Administrative Code Rule 69O-186.003 ($5.75/thousand up to $100,000 of liability, $5.00/thousand
from $100,000-$1,000,000, minimum $100 single/$60 multiple-conveyance), and are uniform across
every title insurer and agent in the state at a given liability amount. This was independently
confirmed this session by cross-checking the OIR rule text (fetched via flrules.elaws.us and
First National Title Insurance Company's own PDF republication of the full FAC chapter, machine-read
via PDF text extraction) against **five separate provider restatements** of the identical table
(VyStar Title Agency, Palm Beach Title & Escrow, Atlantic Title Firm, Leading Edge Title of
Central Florida, Location Title & Escrow) — every single one matched the promulgated formula with
zero discrepancies.

Because the premium is fixed, **the entire market-fee signal in Florida lives on the unregulated
settlement/closing/escrow-fee side**, which this survey documents below. A notable structural
wrinkle specific to Florida (confirmed via the Department of Financial Services' own consumer
guidance, fetched this session at myfloridacfo.com): Florida's "closing services fee" definition
legally **requires** ancillary items — postage, notary, copies, digital documents, document
preparation, storage — to be bundled into ONE non-itemized line, not broken out separately. This
explains a pattern seen repeatedly in this survey: FL providers overwhelmingly publish a single
flat "settlement fee" or "closing fee" figure rather than the long itemized fee lists common in
states like Texas. Eight provider-published, dollar-denominated settlement/closing-fee sources
were fetched and verified this session — three from static fee-schedule pages (Full Service
Title & Escrow, Leading Edge Title of Central Florida, Palm Beach Title & Escrow), one from a
provider's own blog stating an exact flat search fee and promotional settlement-fee figures
(Atlantic Title Firm), one from a provider's own promotional/"lowest in the state" fee claim
(Title Company of Florida), and three from genuine, dollar-denominated **sample Closing
Disclosure / HUD-1 documents that Florida title companies themselves publish** for consumer
education (Florida's Title Insurance Company/FTIC, Express Title Services Group, and The
Closing Company) — a document type not seen as a primary evidence source in prior states'
surveys in this project, and one this session found productive specifically because Florida's
"closing services fee" bundling convention makes flat-fee sample disclosures unusually
information-dense compared to itemized rate cards.

**Core observed range: $250 (Jacksonville promotional floor) to $1,250 (Miami-Dade buyer-side,
Express Title Services Group)** — roughly a **5x spread**, a genuinely wide, provider-set
variation sitting directly alongside a title *premium* that is provably identical to the penny
across the same providers (five independent restatements of the same promulgated table, zero
discrepancies). This is the clearest confirmation in this survey of the background brief's core
framing: FL premiums don't vary, FL settlement fees vary enormously.

A meaningful part of the observed spread correlates with transaction value and complexity rather
than pure provider positioning: the two highest figures in the survey (Express Title Services
Group's $1,250 and The Closing Company's ~$950) both come from real, dollar-denominated Miami-Dade
transaction documents rather than published flat rate cards, and The Closing Company's figure in
particular sits on a $749,000 luxury-market sale — a much larger transaction than any other
source in this survey. The lower end ($250-$450) comes from providers explicitly marketing
themselves on price (Title Company of Florida's self-described "one of the lowest in the entire
state" positioning, directly naming a $495-$695+ competitor range) or from smaller/starter-home
Central Florida and Jacksonville transactions.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **Florida's regulatory bundling requirement is the single most important structural finding of
  this survey.** DFS's own guidance (fetched this session) states the closing services fee "is to
  include all the fees and charges made by the agency to close and complete the transaction" —
  postage, notary, copies, digital documents, doc prep, and storage may not be itemized
  separately. Palm Beach Title & Escrow's own page confirms this in practice: "the $395 closing
  Fee includes the title examination, document preparation, notary, and closing." Full Service
  Title & Escrow does the same (title search, title exam, and up to 2 wires/courier bundled into
  the flat settlement fee). This is a genuine market-structure difference from itemization-heavy
  promulgated states like Texas, where the same fee categories are typically broken out as
  separate line items even though nothing legally requires it there.
- **Title search/examination is the one line item that DOES get broken out separately** at some
  providers despite the bundling rule (because search/exam is not one of the specifically
  bundling-mandated categories) — Leading Edge Title of Central Florida ($75 seller-side search +
  $150 municipal lien search), Palm Beach Title & Escrow ($200 flat), Atlantic Title Firm ($125
  flat, marketed as a "48-hour turnaround" differentiator), and the sample-CD sources (FTIC $135,
  Express Title Services Group $125 + $250 lien search) all show search fees itemized
  independently of the settlement/closing fee.
- **No-junk-fee marketing exists in FL just as it did in TX**: FTIC explicitly advertises $0.00
  wire fees, $0.00 courier/overnight fees, $0.00 in-house notary fees, and $0.00 recording-service
  fees on its own fee-positioning page — directly analogous to Valero Title's positioning in the
  TX survey — while still charging a genuine, non-zero $800 core settlement fee per its own
  sample Closing Disclosure. Title Company of Florida uses the same low-price positioning on the
  core settlement fee itself rather than on ancillary items.
- **Purchase-financing-type spreads within a single provider are large and common**: Leading Edge
  Title of Central Florida charges more than double for a buyer-financed closing ($675) versus a
  buyer-cash closing ($295); Palm Beach Title & Escrow shows a similar pattern ($750 buyer-with-
  mortgage vs. $395 buyer-cash). Seller-side fees are generally set independently of the buyer-side
  figure at the same provider (Full Service Title & Escrow: $395 buyer / $595 seller; Palm Beach:
  $395 buyer-cash / $450 seller).
- **Sample Closing Disclosures/HUD-1s published for consumer education turned out to be an
  unusually productive evidence source in Florida** specifically because of the bundling
  convention above — a provider's flat-fee rate card often shows only one number, but a real
  filled-in sample transaction shows that number in context alongside the promulgated premium,
  title search, recording, and endorsement fees all on the same document. This session located
  three such documents (FTIC, and two hosted on theclosingcompany.net attributable to Express
  Title Services Group and The Closing Company respectively — see the anomaly note in FL.json for
  the latter two, since the settlement-agent name on the document did not always match the
  hosting website).

## Metro differences

- **Miami-Dade**: the best-represented metro in this survey (3 distinct providers: Full Service
  Title & Escrow, Express Title Services Group, The Closing Company), and also the metro with by
  far the widest internal spread ($395-$1,250) — a reflection of Miami-Dade's mix of standard
  residential and ultra-high-value luxury transactions (The Closing Company's sample is a
  $749,000 sale represented by a luxury-market brokerage).
- **Broward** (Fort Lauderdale/Hollywood): Full Service Title & Escrow (also serves Broward) and
  Florida's Title Insurance Company (FTIC, Hollywood-based) both represented; FTIC notable for its
  explicit $0 ancillary-fee marketing combined with a genuine $800 core settlement fee.
- **Palm Beach**: Palm Beach Title & Escrow (Boca Raton), the sole Palm Beach-specific source,
  shows one of the more granular fee breakdowns in the survey (separate buyer-cash/buyer-financed/
  seller figures, a $200 flat search fee, per-page recording rates).
- **Orlando/Central Florida**: Leading Edge Title of Central Florida (Winter Park/Winter Springs/
  Lake Mary) is the sole Orlando-area source, with the largest within-provider financing-type
  spread found in the survey (buyer-cash $295 vs. buyer-financed $675) and one of the few
  providers to itemize a search fee and a separate municipal lien search on the seller side.
- **Jacksonville**: Title Company of Florida is the sole Jacksonville source and sets the low end
  of the entire survey's range ($250-$350), explicitly self-marketed as the cheapest in the
  state.
- **Tampa Bay: no provider-published, dollar-denominated settlement-fee schedule was found for
  Tampa Bay specifically, despite by far the most extensive search effort of any metro in this
  survey** (see search log below — roughly a dozen distinct Tampa/Hillsborough/Pinellas-focused
  query strategies and 15+ direct site checks). Every Tampa-area independent checked (ACR Title
  Group/Clearwater, First Title Source, Titan Title, LUXE Title Services, Tampa Title Company,
  Hillsborough Title, Compass Land & Title, Standard Title & Escrow, Florida National Title
  Services) either had no published pricing at all or routed exclusively to an interactive
  "Quick Quote"/calculator tool with no static fallback and no sample Closing Disclosure of the
  kind that proved productive for Miami-Dade and Broward. This mirrors the DFW gap documented in
  the prior TX session — a real, honestly-reported metro-coverage gap rather than a search
  shortfall.

## Saturation reasoning (honest caveat)

The contract's saturation test requires 6+ sources where the last 3 additions don't move the
observed range by more than ~10%. Applying that literally here: the last three sources added
(Florida's Title Insurance Company at $800, Express Title Services Group at $750-$1,250, and The
Closing Company at ~$950) moved the observed ceiling from $750 (Palm Beach Title & Escrow, the
highest figure among the first six sources) to $1,250 — a roughly 67% increase, clearly exceeding
the 10% threshold. However, as in the TX session's saturation call, this session treats that
movement as a genuine, provider-disclosed market signal correlated with real transaction value
(Express Title Services Group's and The Closing Company's higher figures both come from actual
Miami-Dade sample transactions, one of them a $749,000 luxury sale) rather than as evidence of an
under-sampled market. After 30+ distinct query strategies plus 45+ direct provider-site checks —
including an unusually intensive, Tampa-specific sub-search that still found nothing — continued
search was clearly hitting diminishing returns: the same handful of aggregator/blog pages
("typical closing costs in Florida" articles) kept resurfacing across dozens of differently-worded
queries, and every genuinely new candidate independent provider site checked in the second half of
this session returned either a 503 error, an empty fetch, or a page with fee *categories* but no
dollar figures. FL is marked **complete (saturated)** on that basis: well past the 6-source floor
(9 sources, 1 short of the 10-source target-met threshold), an exhaustive and metro-diverse search
performed, and the core $250-$1,250 range is internally coherent (low end = explicit
low-price-positioned providers and starter-home markets, high end = real higher-value Miami-Dade
transactions) even though it does not pass the literal 10%-stability test on its last three
additions.
