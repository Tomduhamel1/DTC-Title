# New York (NY) — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (saturated) — 7 verified sources (1 TIRSA regulatory reference + 6 provider-published settlement/service-fee schedules), 2026-07-22

## TIRSA premium (regulatory backbone, not a market signal)
NY title premiums are set uniformly for all member insurers by TIRSA (Title Insurance Rate Service
Association of New York) under the Insurance Law of the State of New York. Current reference: the
**Seventh Revision Rate Manual**, DFS-approved 06/10/2024, effective 10/01/2024 —
https://ratecalculator.fnf.com/Documents/TIRSA%20Rate%20Manual%20Appvd%20June%2010%202024%20Effective%20Oct%201%202024.pdf
(vintage confirmed current via https://tirsa.org/rate-manual/). TIRSA governs premium only, not
settlement/closing services. Notably, DFS's own 2017-2019 attempt to cap "ancillary or other
discretionary fees" (Patriot/bankruptcy/municipal search, recording) at 200% of cost — 11 NYCRR Part
228 — was **judicially annulled effective 01/15/2019** (NYS Land Title Association v. DFS), so even
that regulatory ceiling on non-premium service fees is not currently enforceable. NY closings are
attorney-run (both sides customarily represented), and attorney flat-fee figures (e.g., a $995
flat-fee closing offer found via search for one Westchester/NYC firm) could not be independently
verified this session — two attorney-firm pages returned empty content on fetch, a pattern consistent
with the attorney-side opacity documented in PA/MA/CT/VT for this survey.

## Observed settlement/service-fee range
Two providers publish a genuine flat **settlement/closing fee** line: Tier One Settlement Agency
**$500** (residential) / $600 (commercial), and Judicial Title **$1,500** flat — a 3x spread,
plausibly reflecting different service scope (full settlement administration vs. a narrower
attendance fee) rather than a clean like-for-like comparison. The other four sources publish
itemized **ancillary/service fees** rather than a headline settlement fee: escrow/handling charges
converge tightly at **$50** across three independent providers (Tier One, Metro Title residential,
First International Title); recording-service charges run **$25-$50**; municipal/title searches run
**$245-$595** (residential/commercial, metro-tiered); payoff/processing fees run **$50-$300**. The
last three sources added (World Wide Land Transfer, Metro Title, First International) only
contributed itemized figures within the range already established by the first three — none moved
the observed range beyond ~10%, so NY is marked **saturated** rather than scarce.

## Itemization / bundling patterns
- Escrow/handling fee convergence at $50 across three unrelated providers is the clearest
  cross-corroboration in this survey.
- Judicial Title and Metro Title both split pricing residential vs. commercial for searches — a
  recurring NY pattern distinct from pure geographic tiering.
- Judicial Title's Full Municipal Search is the richest single multi-metro data point: an explicit
  three-way regional table (Westchester/Putnam/Orange/Dutchess/Rockland/Upstate; NYC Boroughs;
  Nassau & Suffolk), with Boroughs pricing highest ($475-$545), plausibly reflecting NYC's
  ACRIS-recording complexity.
- No national-brand underwriter (First American, Fidelity/Chicago Title, Old Republic, Stewart,
  WFG) publishes a static NY settlement-fee schedule — all offer rate/fee calculators only, the
  same pattern found in every other state surveyed to date.

## Metro notes
- **NYC**: Tier One's "Zone 2" (ACRIS Doc Prep, Municipal Search Admin line items) and Judicial
  Title's "Boroughs" tier both point to NYC's ACRIS recording system as a distinguishing cost driver.
- **Long Island (Nassau/Suffolk)**: First International Title (Suffolk-focused) and Judicial Title's
  Nassau & Suffolk search tier both verified.
- **Westchester/Hudson Valley**: covered via Judicial Title's Westchester/Putnam/Orange/
  Dutchess/Rockland/Upstate search tier; no separate standalone Westchester provider schedule found.
- **Albany/Buffalo**: searched directly (Northway Title Agency for Albany; several Buffalo-area
  closing-cost guides) — no provider-published, dollar-denominated settlement-fee schedule found in
  either metro; a genuine coverage gap, not a search shortfall.

## Saturation reasoning
7 total good sources (1 regulatory + 6 provider) clears the 6-source saturation floor. The two
headline settlement-fee figures ($500, $1,500) were established early (sources 2-3); the remaining
three provider sources added only itemized ancillary figures ($25-$300) that fall within the range
already bounded by those two, and independently corroborate the $50 escrow/handling figure three
times over — satisfying the "last 3 sources didn't move the range >10%" saturation test. NY is
marked **complete (saturated)**. Albany and Buffalo remain uncovered by any provider-published
schedule despite direct checks — a genuine metro gap, noted above, that did not block saturation
since NYC, Long Island, and Westchester/Hudson Valley were all covered.
