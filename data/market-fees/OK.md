# Oklahoma (OK) — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: COMPLETE (saturated) — 7 documents / 4 providers verified

7 documents verified across 4 providers: American Eagle Title Group (3
vintages: 2019, 2023, 2024 -- an independent settlement/title company
serving both Oklahoma City and Tulsa), Old Republic Title (2 vintages:
2019, 2024 -- Oklahoma City/Canadian/Cleveland Counties, each pairing a
genuine itemized settlement-fee schedule with a companion premium
schedule), First National Title Insurance Company (2020, premium-only),
and WFG National Title Insurance Company (2000-vintage manual, unusually
including dollar-denominated ancillary fees alongside its premium chart).
This is a notably rich state: 2 of 4 providers (AETG, Old Republic)
publish genuine, itemized, non-premium settlement-fee schedules rather
than the premium-only pattern typical of most surveyed states. Marked
**complete (saturated)**.

## Key market structure finding

Oklahoma is a "Use and File" rate state (36 O.S. §987): insurers must file
rates with the Oklahoma Insurance Department no later than 30 days after
the effective date, and the OID lacks authority to disapprove rates or
rate changes except in narrow circumstances -- effectively an unregulated/
insurer-set premium market similar to Arkansas. Unlike premiums,
settlement/closing/escrow fees are entirely unregulated and left to each
title company, yet in Oklahoma (unusually among states surveyed) both
independent settlement companies (AETG) and a national-brand underwriter's
direct escrow offices (Old Republic) publish detailed, itemized,
dollar-denominated fee sheets rather than routing to interactive
calculators only -- a meaningfully more transparent market than most
other "unregulated fee" states in this survey.

## Observed service-stack range

Using the 2018-2024 vintage documents (AETG x3, Old Republic x2) as the
current, representative basis: combined buyer+seller settlement-fee
stacks range from roughly **$1,100 (AETG 2019 purchase, borrower fees
only) to $1,775 (AETG 2024 purchase, with all applicable add-ons)** for a
Tulsa transaction, and from **~$1,325 (Old Republic 2019, buyer+seller
combined at $150,000) to ~$1,650 (Old Republic 2024, buyer+seller
combined)** for an Oklahoma City-metro transaction -- a reasonably stable,
comparable range across both metros and both 5-year vintage spans.

**Methodology note (matching the precedent set for GA's REO exclusion and
HI's timeshare exclusion):** WFG's 2000-vintage figures ($100 exam + $100
report + $50 simultaneous + $300-550 abstracting, totaling roughly
$450-$700) are excluded from this range calculation. Given the document's
25-year-old effective date and the absence of any confirmed re-filing,
these figures are not treated as representative of current Oklahoma
market pricing, even though the document itself is the only current WFG
OK filing indexed and is included in the evidence file for completeness.

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- Both AETG and Old Republic split settlement pricing into a similar core
  structure: a flat Closing Fee, a separate Title Exam fee, a separate
  Final (Title) Report/Search fee, and a separate Title Service(s) Fee --
  a 4-component breakdown that recurs consistently across all 5
  settlement-fee documents in this state, unusually structured compared
  to most other states' single bundled closing fee.
- Abstracting is explicitly called out by both AETG and Old Republic as
  set/maintained by third-party county abstract companies, not the title
  company itself -- Old Republic's Chart B (2019 vintage) ties the
  abstracting fee to the *age* of the prior certification (5 tiers, $300
  to $550/$500-550), a distinctive age-based-abstract-currency pricing
  mechanism not seen in other states surveyed.
- Both AETG and Old Republic price a Document Prep fee ($75) and Notary
  Signing fee ($150) identically as "additional fees, if applicable" --
  a clean cross-provider corroboration on these two ancillary charges.
- Old Republic's rate cards are the only documents in this survey found
  to include a fully worked numeric example allocating every single fee
  line item between buyer and seller for a specific sale price -- more
  explicit than the general "customary payer" language seen in most
  other states' documents.
- Neither FNTI's nor (in its premium chart) WFG's manual prices a CPL fee
  for Oklahoma -- a coverage gap for this data point in this state.

the same document.

## Metro differences

AETG's fee sheets cover both Oklahoma City and Tulsa under one set of
statewide figures (identical pricing shown for both cities in the 2019
vintage); Old Republic's fee sheets are scoped specifically to Oklahoma
City metro (Oklahoma, Canadian, Cleveland Counties) with Tulsa not
separately addressed by Old Republic. No provider in this state was found
to price Tulsa and Oklahoma City differently from each other -- unlike
many other states' urban/rural differentials, Oklahoma's two largest
metros appear to be priced uniformly by the providers surveyed.
