# North Carolina (NC) Title / Closing / Settlement Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (saturated) — 6 verified sources, 2026-07-21

North Carolina meets the completion contract's saturation bar: 6 independently verified,
directly-fetched published sources. Of these, 4 (Chicago Title, NC Title Services,
Barristers Title, Investors Title) are NCTIRB (North Carolina Title Insurance Rating
Bureau) member republications of the **same bureau-uniform premium rate table** —
confirming NC's premium is not shoppable, exactly as the completion contract anticipated
for a rating-bureau state. The 3 most recently added sources (NC Title Services,

settlement/closing service fee, so they do not move the observed service-stack range at

## Settlement/service fee range actually observed

Only **24 Hour Closing** publishes a full settlement-fee stack; **Cline Donaldson**
explicitly declines to publish theirs; the 4 NCTIRB sources price title premium only
(by design — NC premiums are not a service-fee shopping target, per the bureau-uniform
structure).

- **24 Hour Closing**: $650 (financed purchase/refinance) / $550 (cash purchase) closing
  fee + $375 title search/final title fee + $150 admin fee = **$1,175** base stack on a
  financed purchase, **before** a $250-per-additional-$100,000 escalator above a
  $500,000 purchase price (e.g., a $700k purchase adds $500, bringing the stack to
  ~$1,675) and before add-ons (mail-away $200, second loan closing $450, curative work
  $500-$1,000).
- **Cline Donaldson**: settlement/closing fee is deliberately unpublished. The firm does
  publish ancillary items: $50 overnight/courier fee, $10-$150 e-recording vendor fee,
  $300 mobile notary, $150 power-of-attorney prep — but the core attorney fee requires
  contacting the firm directly, a genuine market-opacity finding (not every NC closing
  attorney publishes settlement pricing, unlike Georgia's closing-attorney firms which
  publish itemized fee sheets as standard practice).

**Observed range: single confirmed data point, $1,175 (24 Hour Closing base stack) up to
roughly $1,675+ on higher-value purchases with the per-$100k escalator.** This is thinner
than other states' settlement-fee ranges because most NC providers found publish premium
rates only, not service fees — itself a notable market finding (contrast with Georgia,
where every closing-attorney firm found published itemized service fees).

## Itemization / bundling patterns

- **NCTIRB bureau uniformity is exceptionally strong**: 4 independent companies
  (Chicago Title, NC Title Services, Barristers Title, Investors Title) all quote
  *identical* premium tiers, minimum premiums, and simultaneous-issue charges at any
  given point in time — NC's title insurance premium is genuinely non-shoppable, the
  strongest confirmation of rating-bureau uniformity found in any state surveyed.
- Bureau rates cover **title insurance premium only** — none of the 4 bureau sources
  publish closing/settlement, title search, document prep, courier, wire, or notary
  fees, consistent with the bureau's own boilerplate ("filed rates... encompass all the
  premiums for title insurance," implicitly excluding settlement services).
- Where settlement fees ARE published (24 Hour Closing only), the structure is itemized
  into separate line items (closing fee, search fee, admin fee) rather than one bundled
  number, similar to the pattern seen in Georgia and California.
- **Recording fees are one of the few figures that show slight cross-source variation**:
  24 Hour Closing quotes deed $26 / deed of trust $64; Cline Donaldson references the
  same $26/$64 figures — consistent recording costs, likely reflecting NC's statutory
  per-page recording fee structure rather than company-set pricing.
