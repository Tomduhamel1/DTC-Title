# New Jersey (NJ) — Market Fee Evidence

## Status: complete (scarce market) — 5 verified sources (1 rating-bureau regulatory manual + 4 provider-published), 2026-07-22

## NJLTIRB premium — and, uniquely, settlement fee — note (regulatory backbone)
NJ title insurance premiums are set uniformly for all member insurers/agents by the **New Jersey
Land Title Insurance Rating Bureau (NJLTIRB)**, an unincorporated rating organization operating
under N.J.S.A. 17:46B-46 pursuant to the Title Insurance Act of 1974. Current reference: the
**Manual of Rates and Charges, effective November 1, 2023** (applies to all applications/orders
received on or after that date; no newer vintage located as of retrieval) —
https://cdn.ymaws.com/njlta.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/docs/rate_manual_-_11-01-2023.pdf, hosted
as a courtesy copy by the New Jersey Land Title Association (njlta.org/page/RateManual, which
403-blocked direct fetch but confirmed the same vintage via search-snippet). Standard Underwriting
Rate: $5.25/thousand up to $100,000, +$4.25/thousand to $500,000, +$2.75/thousand to $2 million,
+$2.00/thousand above that ($200,000 liability computes to $950.00); $200 minimum charge.

**The central structural finding of this survey**: unlike PA (TIRBOP) and NY (TIRSA), NJLTIRB does
not stop at the premium — its **Article 6 ("Settlement Charges") directly promulgates the
settlement/closing fee itself**: $300.00 flat for a settlement with disbursements (in-office,
business hours, ≤60 min), $150.00 without disbursements, with fixed surcharges (+$25 off-site,
+$50 after-hours, +$100/hr or +$50/hr overtime). This is not a market signal — it is a
rate-regulated number every NJLTIRB member must charge, and this survey independently confirmed it
byte-for-byte identical across two provider-published fee schedules (World Wide Land Transfer's NJ
page and Federated National Land LLC's schedule). The CPL is likewise fixed at $75.00 (Article 6.6).

## Where the real (unregulated) market signal lives
NJLTIRB's Article 7.6 explicitly carves out a narrow set of categories as **cost-based, not
Manual-fixed**: wire transfer fees, statutory notary fees, and third-party/lender
platform-compliance fees ("the Insurer or Title Insurance Agent may charge the cost for such
services"). This is the actual unregulated territory the task brief targeted, and it is
considerably narrower in NJ than in most other states surveyed (courier/overnight is also
cost-based per Article 7.5; e-doc printing, by contrast, **is** fixed at $25/set, $50 cap, per
Article 7.2.2). This survey found genuine provider variation exactly in the unregulated wire-fee
line: **$15.00 (Coastal Title Agency) vs. $25.00 (Federated National Land LLC)** — a real,
if narrow, cross-provider settlement-adjacent data point.

Separately, NJ's Northern-county custom of both buyer and seller retaining independent attorneys
(not statutorily mandated, per the task brief) produces a second, additive fee layer on top of the
regulated title/settlement charge: **attorney flat fees of $995 (Y. Levin Law, statewide) to
$2,000 (Michael Makarov, 15 counties)** — roughly a 2x spread, and each several multiples of the
$300 regulated settlement-fee base. Coastal Title Agency's own published "$525 Settlement" line is
notably above the $300 regulated base; the page does not itemize what the $525 bundles, so this is
flagged as an open question rather than resolved.

## Itemization / bundling patterns
- NJLTIRB is the only rating bureau found in this survey to date that promulgates the settlement
  fee as a fixed number (not just the premium) — a stronger regulatory reach than PA's
  Company/Agent-only bundling or NY's premium-only TIRSA structure.
- The Manual's fixed/unregulated line is drawn narrowly and specifically: settlement attendance,
  CPL, e-doc printing, and most miscellaneous recording-adjacent charges (mortgage satisfaction
  $75, subordination $35, legal description $50, duplicate policy $25, pro forma $250) are all
  fixed by the Manual; only wire fees, notary fees, and lender/platform compliance fees are
  left to the market (Article 7.6).
- Two attorney-fee sources (Makarov $2,000, Y. Levin $995) are additive to, not inclusive of, the
  title company's regulated settlement charge — attorneys explicitly exclude "title company"
  charges from their flat fee.
- No national-brand underwriter (First American, Fidelity/Chicago Title, Old Republic, Stewart,
  WFG) publishes a static NJ settlement-fee schedule — all route to interactive rate/fee
  calculators, the same pattern found in every other state surveyed to date.

## Metro notes
- **Northern NJ / NYC suburbs (Bergen/Essex/Hudson)**: best-covered metro — Coastal Title Agency
  (Jersey City/Hudson satellite office), Federated National Land LLC (NYC-based, NY/NJ dual
  practice), and both attorney sources (Makarov and Y. Levin explicitly list Bergen/Essex/Hudson).
  Additional Bergen-area attorney firms found via search (Flat Rate Legal, Peter J. Lamont, Joseph
  A. DiPiazza) all advertise "flat-fee" closings qualitatively but none published a dollar figure
  on the pages checked — a genuine market-opacity finding on the attorney-marketing side, distinct
  from the two firms that do quote a number.
- **Central NJ (Middlesex/Mercer/Princeton)**: covered only via the two statewide/multi-county
  attorney sources (Makarov lists Mercer/Middlesex/Monmouth/Hunterdon/Somerset; Y. Levin is
  statewide) — **no title-company-specific settlement-fee schedule was found for this metro**
  despite direct searches; Two Rivers Title's Princeton/Mercer County pages were identified but are
  quote-only (consistent with this provider's behavior documented in the NY survey).
- **Southern NJ (Camden/Atlantic/Cape May)**: covered only by Y. Levin Law's explicit statewide
  county list (which names Atlantic, Camden, Cape May, Cumberland, Gloucester, Salem) — **no South
  Jersey title company was found publishing dollar figures** despite direct checks of Cape Atlantic
  Title Agency (Linwood/Atlantic County), SJS Title/South Jersey Settlement Agency (Cherry Hill),
  Homestead Title Agency (Cherry Hill/Atlantic City, DNS failure on fetch), and National Integrity
  Title (Marlton) — all describe services qualitatively with no pricing, a genuine metro-coverage
  gap on the title-company side specifically (the task brief's noted South Jersey norm of
  title-company-run rather than attorney-run closings makes this gap notable).

## Saturation/scarcity reasoning
This session located **5 good sources** (1 regulatory + 4 provider-published), below the 6-source
saturation floor, despite **11 distinct query strategies and 14 direct provider-site checks** —
comfortably past the 8-strategy exhaustive-search threshold required to invoke the scarce-market
provision. NJ's structural characteristics plausibly explain the thinness of provider-side evidence
specifically: (1) NJLTIRB's direct regulation of the settlement fee itself removes the ordinary
commercial incentive to publish a standalone, differentiated settlement-fee number, since by law
it is the same $300/$150 figure at every title company/agent (the two provider schedules that do
publish it simply restate the regulatory number verbatim rather than compete on it); (2) the
genuinely unregulated categories (wire, notary, courier) are narrow enough, and small enough in
dollar terms, that most providers do not bother publishing them separately (only 2 of the 4
verified provider sources itemize wire fees at all); and (3) the attorney layer, where used, tends
toward "flat fee, quote-only-beyond-that" marketing rather than itemized schedules — 4 of 6 named
Bergen-area attorney firms checked (Flat Rate Legal, Peter J. Lamont, Joseph A. DiPiazza, plus
2 blocked/unresolved fetches) advertise "flat fee" without a public number, and South Jersey title
companies (Cape Atlantic, SJS Title, Homestead, National Integrity) uniformly publish no pricing.
NJ is marked **complete (scarce market)** on that basis. The clearest positive finding of this
session is, as with PA, a structural one rather than a pricing range: NJLTIRB's Manual Article 6
is the most direct settlement-fee-promulgation mechanism found in this survey to date (more direct
than PA's TIRBOP, which only bundles settlement into the premium under one of two procedures) —
meaning NJ's title-side settlement fee is regulatory, not market, evidence, while the market
signal that does exist is concentrated in the narrow wire-fee ($15 vs. $25) and attorney-fee
($995 vs. $2,000) data points documented above.
