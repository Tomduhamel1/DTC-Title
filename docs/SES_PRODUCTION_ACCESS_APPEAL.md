# SES production-access appeal — BetterClose

**Status:** account `621852467690`, us-east-1, is in the SES **sandbox**.
`ProductionAccessEnabled: false`; prior request **DENIED**, case
`177722805500436`. Sending identity `betterclose.co` is verified with DKIM.

**Impact:** launch-blocking. In the sandbox SES only delivers to verified
identities, so magic-link sign-in fails for every real user — brokers,
agents, lenders — with NextAuth's opaque `EmailSignin` error. Individually
verifying an address (`aws ses verify-email-identity`) is the only
workaround and does not scale past internal testers.

## How to submit (do this in the console, not the CLI)

The account details AWS already has are correct — `MailType: TRANSACTIONAL`,
`WebsiteURL: https://www.betterclose.co`, `EnforcementStatus: HEALTHY`. So
re-running `aws sesv2 put-account-details` would resubmit *the same
information that was already denied*. The appeal has to carry the
justification below, which means a support case.

**Preferred path — follow up on the existing case.** Reopen case
`177722805500436` in the AWS Support Center and post the appeal text as a
reply. Following up on the original case keeps the history together and is
what AWS support suggests for a denial.

**If the case can't be reopened** (older cases sometimes lock), open a new
case: Service = *Amazon Simple Email Service*, Category = *Sending
Limits / Request production access*. Reference case `177722805500436`
explicitly and paste the same text.

Console: https://console.aws.amazon.com/support/home#/case/create
SES account dashboard: https://console.aws.amazon.com/ses/home#/account

AWS provides an initial response within ~24 hours. Note that they may ask
for a period of clean sandbox sending history before granting access, so
submit sooner rather than later — this is on the critical path to launch.

## Appeal text (paste into the support case)

**Use case.** BetterClose is a title-and-settlement platform at
https://www.betterclose.co. We send **transactional email only**. There is
no marketing, newsletter, promotional, or bulk mail of any kind, and no
purchased, rented, scraped, or imported lists.

**What we send, and what triggers it.** Every message is generated by an
action the recipient just took:

1. **Magic-link sign-in.** The user types their own email address into our
   sign-in form and requests a one-time link. The email goes only to the
   address just entered, and the link expires shortly after issuance. This
   is our primary and highest-volume message.
2. **Closing-file notifications.** Sent to parties on a transaction they are
   already a participant in (their own closing), triggered by document or
   status events on that file.
3. **Partner referral confirmations.** Sent to a counterparty a signed-in
   user explicitly referred as part of a transaction.

Recipients are therefore always either the person who just typed the
address, or a named party to a real estate transaction that is already
under way. We never email an address that has not initiated contact.

**Bounce and complaint handling.** SES account-level suppression is enabled
for both `BOUNCE` and `COMPLAINT`. We send from a verified domain
(`betterclose.co`) with DKIM verified, and use a consistent `noreply@`
sender. Because all mail is transactional and recipient-initiated, we do
not operate mailing lists; the equivalent of unsubscribe is simply that no
further mail is generated unless the user acts again. We monitor sending
through CloudWatch and will act on any bounce or complaint signal.

**Volume.** Low and proportional to sign-ins — on the order of tens of
messages per day at launch, growing with active users. We are not
requesting a large quota, only production access so that legitimate users
can receive their own sign-in links.

**Why the previous request may have been denied.** Our earlier submission
did not spell out the recipient-consent model or our bounce/complaint
posture. This appeal supplies both: every recipient is the initiator of the
message or a named party to their own transaction, and suppression is
already enabled at the account level.

## After access is granted

1. Re-test production sign-in end to end with a non-verified outside
   address; expect a redirect to `verify-request`, not
   `error=EmailSignin`.
2. Remove any one-off verified identities added as sandbox workarounds
   (e.g. `steve@firstnte.com`) once they are no longer needed.
3. Consider surfacing a clearer error than NextAuth's generic
   `EmailSignin` when a send fails, so a future outage is diagnosable from
   the UI rather than from CloudWatch. The failure mode that cost us time
   here was that *every* cause — bad from-address, IAM denial, sandbox
   restriction — presented identically as "Could not send email."
